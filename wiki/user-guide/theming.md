# Theming

Reading is personal. In diffwiki the author writes the content, but **how you read it is your choice** — customizable like your editor's colorscheme. Two people reading the same published wiki can read it in completely different themes, and neither changes the other's experience or the underlying markdown.

## Pick a theme

Open the settings dialog (the gear in the top navigation) to choose from 30+ built-in themes — `default`, `catppuccin`, `claude`, `graphite`, `cyberpunk`, `nature`, `mono`, and many more. Each theme is a full look: it sets the color palette **and** the typography (its own sans, serif, mono, and heading fonts), so switching themes restyles the whole reading surface at once. A live swatch previews each theme in its own colors before you apply it.

## Light, dark, or system

Independently of the theme, choose a color mode: **Light**, **Dark**, or **System**. System follows your operating system's appearance and updates live when your OS flips between light and dark.

## Your preferences, not the author's

Theme and mode are stored locally in your browser — they are a per-reader setting, never baked into the published site. This is the point: your reading experience belongs to you, the way an editor's theme belongs to you, not to the person who wrote the file.

More reading customization is on the way — remembered per-reader state, and keyboard-driven navigation with full Helix key bindings. See the [Roadmap](/whats-next#roadmap).
